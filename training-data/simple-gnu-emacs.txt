GNU Emacs short for Editing Macros is thought to be text editor that is common on many UNIX based operating systems Mac and Windows Operating systems but it's actually a extendable elisp editor that can be made to be just about anything Emacs is primarily used by just about everyone from programmers to home desktop users. This is because emacs can do just about anything you can think of. Some of the uses of Emacs include Creating PDF documents Setting up agendas and to do lists Reading e mail and news Keeping a calendar and diary Chat on IRC It can even act as a desktop manger Linux BSD or other Unix like systems only at this time Emacs is made powerful by Emacs Lisp a built in programming language that lets the user extend the capabilities of the editor. A common Emacs joke is that all of the functions of the editor are crazy weird keystrokes such as control meta shift left P semicolon F to do something simple like cut and paste text . In reality though these keystrokes are relatively simple though they can take some getting used to. There is an Internet turf war between programmers that prefer Emacs and programmers that prefer Vim or Vi another common text editor.
