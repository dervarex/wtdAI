Kazakh text Kazakh is a Turkic language that is mostly spoken in Kazakhstan a country in Central Asia. It along with Russian is one of Kazakhstan's official languages. The Kazakh language had been written in the Cyrillic alphabet since the Russian Empire started to occupy that country in the th century. In people began to use the Arabic alphabet but the Russians were not able to make them use Cyrillic. The Kazakhs used the Arabic script to write their language until approximately . In the early s Kazakh activist Ahmed Baytursinuli reformed the Kazakh Arabic alphabet but his work was largely overshadowed by the Soviet presence in Central Asia. At that point the new Soviet regime forced the Kazakhs to use a Latin script and then a Cyrillic script in the s in an effort to thoroughly Russify them. Today Kazakhs use the Arabic Latin and Cyrillic scripts to write their language. Following the dissolution of the Soviet Union in the newly independent Republic of Kazakhstan allowed the old Latin alphabet to be used but continued to use Cyrillic as the main alphabet. In president Nursultan Nazarbayev started a project to introduce a standard form of the Latin alphabet as the official script of his country. A version of the new Latin alphabet which used apostrophes for certain sounds was created in April but was replaced with another one in February which instead uses diacritics on certain letters. The latest version proposed in has been in official use and will be taught in elementary schools starting . If all goes as planned the Cyrillic alphabet will be fully replaced with Latin by .
