ACME Night was a weekly American programming block. It premiered on September on Cartoon Network September August and on Adult Swim September December airing on Sunday evenings. It aired animated and live action movies and series. The block was conceived by Warner Bros. Discovery then WarnerMedia executive Tom Ascheim to encourage co viewing targeting a multi generational audience with family friendly content. It served as a spiritual successor to Flicks a similar movie block that aired on Cartoon Network from to . The branding including its name and original opening bumper featuring Wile E. Coyote and Road Runner was derived from the fictional ACME Corporation from Looney Tunes. On September WarnerMedia Kids Family announced the creation of ACME Night a new weekly programming block scheduled to air every Sunday at p.m. ET PT on Cartoon Network. The block was part of a larger initiative by network president Tom Ascheim to broaden the channel's demographic appeal by fostering co viewing opportunities for children and their parents. Its name was a tribute to the fiction ACME Corporation from the Looney Tunes. The block officially debuted on September with a broadcast of the DC Extended Universe film Shazam . The initial lineup featured a heavy rotation of major theatrical titles including Man of Steel The LEGO Movie The Second Part and Detective Pikachu. Initial broadcasts were distinguished by custom on air branding which included computer animated bumpers featuring Wile E. Coyote and the Road Runner in futuristic desert scenes. These elements were designed to signal the start of the family oriented block before the transition into Adult Swim's nightly sign on. On September the ACME Night block officially moved to Adult Swim following the network's daily sign on expansion to p.m. ET PT. The transition effectively made the block a fully branded Adult Swim product though it continued to air family friendly theatrical and animated films. The first film broadcast during the Adult Swim era was Batman Forever. The block's presentation evolved during its tenure on the channel. By September Adult Swim discontinued the specific ACME Night intro bumpers replacing them with a standard network advisory warning followed immediately by the movie. Throughout the block experienced several hiatuses often being replaced by Futurama marathons or direct to video films for several weeks at a time. ACME Night concluded its three year run on December with a final airing of Charlie and the Chocolate Factory the block ended with the final words ACME Night continues now marking the end to the block. The block was discontinued as part of a schedule revamp that included the return of Family Guy and a shift in programming focus for the p.m. and p.m. hours. Following its closure the Sunday time slot was replaced with reruns of King of the Hill.
