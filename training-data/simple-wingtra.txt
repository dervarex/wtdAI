Wingtra is a Swiss company that develops uncrewed aerial vehicles UAVs and software for professional aerial surveying and mapping. Based in Zurich the company specializes in vertical takeoff and landing VTOL drones that combine the ability to take off and land vertically with the flight efficiency of fixed wing aircraft. Its systems are used to collect aerial imagery and geospatial data for applications including surveying construction mining infrastructure planning agriculture and environmental monitoring. Wingtra originated in robotics research at ETH Zurich's Autonomous Systems Lab in Switzerland. The early project explored an aircraft design that could take off and land vertically like a multirotor drone and then transition to fixed wing flight. The approach was intended to combine the flexibility of vertical takeoff and landing with the longer range flight capabilities of a conventional fixed wing aircraft. The research was initially connected to work by students at ETH Zurich including Basil Weibel Maximilian Boosfeld Elias Kleimann and Sebastian Verling according to TechCrunch. The research team established Wingtra to develop and commercialize the aircraft technology. The project received support from the Gebert R f Foundation and the Wyss Zurich accelerator program which supports the development of technologies originating from research at ETH Zurich and the University of Zurich. Wingtra subsequently focused on applications for aerial surveying and mapping. The company's early commercial aircraft were developed to collect aerial data for uses such as topographic mapping and agricultural monitoring. Wingtra introduced the WingtraOne a VTOL drone designed for surveying and mapping. The aircraft's design allows it to launch and land vertically while using fixed wing flight for aerial data collection over larger areas. In August Wingtra announced the WingtraOne GEN II a second generation version of its mapping drone. The updated system included camera and imaging improvements intended to support large area mapping and three dimensional data collection. Wingtra's products are designed for professional aerial surveying mapping and geospatial data collection. The company's systems combine a VTOL aircraft with cameras or other sensors positioning technology flight planning software and data processing workflows. Unlike multirotor drones which generally remain airborne through the thrust of their rotors Wingtra's tailsitter design transitions to fixed wing flight after takeoff. This configuration is intended to support surveying over large areas while retaining the ability to operate without a runway or a conventional aircraft launch system. The company's drones are used in sectors that require aerial measurements and geographic information including construction and infrastructure mining urban planning land development agriculture and environmental monitoring. TechCrunch reported in that Wingtra's customers included organizations such as NASA and the United States Army Corps of Engineers. Aerial imagery collected by the aircraft can be processed into geospatial products such as orthomosaics topographic maps and three dimensional models. These outputs can support surveying project planning and the monitoring of land and infrastructure. Wingtra sells its aerial surveying systems through an international network of distributors resellers and other partners. In March TechCrunch reported that the company was pursuing international expansion following a US million Series B funding round. The publication also reported that Wingtra had offices in Zurich Switzerland Fort Lauderdale Florida and Zagreb Croatia. In the United States Wingtra has worked with distribution partners to make its surveying drones available to professional customers. In Commercial UAV News reported on the company's partnership with RDO Integrated Controls which was intended to expand availability of the WingtraOne in the U.S. market. The article also described an earlier partnership with Florida based Lengemann Corporation which supplied the aircraft to customers in Florida Georgia and Alabama. In the United Kingdom and Ireland geospatial equipment supplier KOREC announced in November that it had joined Wingtra's distributor network. The agreement covered sales training and support for the WingtraOne and KOREC said it planned to establish a network of Wingtra dealers in the United Kingdom. UK drone supplier Coptrz has also described its partnership with Wingtra and its work supplying the company's mapping and surveying drones to the UK market. Wingtra's official website maintains a directory of partner companies and distributors in different countries. Its partner program distinguishes between dealers distributors and referral partners with roles that can include direct customer sales supply to dealers and referrals to prospective customers. The company's distribution network provides access to customers across multiple geographic markets. Wingtra has partnered with geospatial software providers to support the processing and use of data collected by its drones. In the company announced a partnership with Pix D a photogrammetry software provider. The collaboration combined Wingtra's aerial data collection hardware with software for producing two dimensional maps and three dimensional models from imagery. Wingtra has also worked with geospatial technology companies to support aerial mapping workflows. In DroneDJ reported that the WingtraOne GEN II had been developed with mapping applications in mind and that Wingtra had partnered with Esri and Bentley Systems to support cloud based image processing. The partnership enabled users to process WingtraOne data and view and analyze resulting orthomaps and three dimensional models through DroneDeploy's software.
