Adobe Atmosphere was a computer program for making and exploring D worlds on the Internet. People could build a world on their computer put it on a website and let other people walk through it using a free web browser add on. The program was first made by a company called Attitude Software where it was named D Anarchy. Adobe Systems bought it in and gave it the new name. In a world each person was shown as a character called an avatar. People could talk to each other by typing messages. Worlds were joined together by portals which worked like the links on web pages. Clicking a portal took the user to another world. The program ran only on Microsoft Windows and worked best with the Internet Explorer web browser. Adobe Photoshop Album could turn a set of photos into a D room that people could walk through and look at the pictures on the walls. Adobe showed the first test version of Atmosphere on March . The full version called . came out on October . During the test years the program was free. When the full version came out Adobe began to charge money for the part used to build worlds. In December Adobe stopped making Atmosphere. The company said the choice was based on the market what customers said and its own research. Adobe later added D tools to its Adobe Acrobat program but those were made with technology from a different company not from Atmosphere.
