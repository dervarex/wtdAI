Alphonse Matejka January in St. Gallen Switzerland October in La Chaux de Fonds Switzerland was a famous Occidentalist of Czech origin. The Matejka family came from Vy kovec in Bohemia. Alphonse Matejka's father moved to Switzerland before and became a Swiss citizen in . His mother was Swiss and from St. Gallen. Alphonse was born in St. Gallen on January . He finished school in St. Gallen and became a Swiss citizen. He trained in an embroidery shop called Reichenbach Co and later worked in its Paris branch for ten years. While in Paris he studied foreign languages in the evenings learning Romance and Scandinavian languages Swahili and Russian. He also wrote for a Russian language journal and translated for the Academy of Sciences. Later he worked for a button export company in Z rich and Amsterdam. On July he married Jeanne Bellanger in Paris. In he moved to La Chaux de Fonds Switzerland and worked in the watchmaking industry. After Bellanger died in Matejka stopped his public activities and moved to a nursing home. He passed away on October at age . Matejka supported Pan Europeanism an idea about uniting Europe. He also believed international languages should come from higher civilizations. He followed the economic ideas of Silvio Gesell who promoted economic freedom. Matejka was very interested in foreign languages and wanted to reform German spelling. In Matejka discovered the language Ido a modified version of Esperanto and decided it was better. He never supported Esperanto and instead joined the Ido movement. He was active in Ido societies and became Secretary of the Union for the International Language Ido ULI from to . President of the Swiss Ido Society . President of ULI in after moving to Paris. He worked on Ido publications like Suisian Idisto and Ido Kuriero and became editor in chief of the magazine Progreso in . At first Matejka opposed the language Occidental later called Interlingue . He argued that Ido should be improved instead. He even wrote under fake names like Franko Veramido and Scorpion to debate against Occidental supporters. But in he became frustrated with Ido and switched to Occidental. This caused shock in the Ido community. Matejka then became A member of the Occidental Academy. Interim president and later president . President of the Occidental Union in the s. A representative for Occidental at the International Auxiliary Language Association IALA . For over years he was the editor of Cosmoglotta the main Occidental magazine. He wrote language courses textbooks translations including Anton Chekhov and essays. When the IALA introduced Interlingua in many Occidentalists left. However Matejka saw Interlingua and Interlingue as similar and promoted good relations between both groups. He published his last article in Cosmoglotta in at age . In Matejka helped create the Centre de documentation et d' tude sur la langue internationale CDELI a research center for international languages. He donated many language materials and frequently visited until his death.
