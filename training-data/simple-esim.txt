An eSIM embedded subscriber identity module is a form of SIM card that is used directly into a device onto an eUICC chip. It connects mobile network operators via remote SIM provisioning and users can change mobile network operators without needing to physically swap a SIM from the device. Most mobile phones released in or later have eSIM technology. Users can change plans or cellulars at any time online. In February the Samsung Gear S Classic G was the first device to have an eSIM. In September the iPhone series were the first iPhone models to replace the physical SIM card with eSIM in the United States. On September the iPhone iPhone Pro and iPhone Pro Max were the first iPhone models to replace the physical SIM card with eSIM in conturies such as Bahrain Canada Guam Japan Kuwait Mexico Oman Qatar Saudi Arabia the United Arab Emirates and U.S. Virgin Islands. The iPhone Air was the first iPhone model to have eSIM only in worldwide.
