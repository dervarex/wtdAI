The Data Discman is an device that plays electronic books. It was made by Sony Corporation which was the parent company of Sony Pictures Entertainment. It came out in the Western market in late or early . It was marketed in the United States to college students and international travelers but was only successful in Japan. The Discman product name was first used for Sony's range of portable CD players such as the Sony Discman D which first came out in . The Data Discman allows information to be accessed quickly on a pre recorded disc. This is done using a QWERTY keyboard and the Yes and No keys. Most Data Discman models have a small low resolution grayscale LCD x at first then up to x and in colour for later models a CD drive either Mini CD or full size and a computer that is not very powerful. Early versions of the device cannot play audio CDs. Software is pre recorded and mostly is encyclopedias foreign language dictionaries and novels. Software was usually made using the Sony Electronic Book Authoring System. A DD EX Data Discman is in the permanent collection of the Victoria and Albert Museum. It is displayed in the V A's th Century Gallery. This early model cannot play sound. An updated model the DD EX came out in or . The manual that comes with it gives a copyright date of . Unlike the DD EX the DD EX can also play audio files. The British version comes with two discs one with the Thomson Electronic Directory for April on it and one with the Pocket Interpreter language conversation book for travelers on it. A DD EX was in an exhibition called The Book and Beyond Electronic Publishing and the Art of the Book which was held at the Victoria and Albert Museum in London from April to October . The exhibition also included a CD ROM for the Data Discman which is called The Library of the Future and came out in . The DD EX and DD EX were both in a flip or clamshell design while the flat rectangular design of the DD was closer to later e book readers such as the Amazon Kindle.
