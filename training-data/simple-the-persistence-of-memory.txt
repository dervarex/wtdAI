The Persistence of Memory is a painting by artist Salvador Dal . It was done in . It is one of his most famous paintings. The painting was first shown in . It has been in the Museum of Modern Art MoMA in New York City since . The painting typifies what critics call surrealism . It is easy to recognise and is often used in popular culture. The painting was the first to have a melting pocket watch called a soft watch . Some people thought that the soft watch was inspired by Albert Einstein's theory of relativity. Dal said that the watches were not inspired by the theory of relativity but by Camembert cheese melting in the sun. There is a human in the middle of the painting. The orange clock at the bottom left is covered in ants. Dal often used ants in his paintings as a symbol for death. The Persistence of Memory uses the exactitude of realist painting techniques .The painting looks more like something a person might see in a dream rather than awake. The rocks to the right are supposed to be a tip of the Cap de Creus peninsula in north eastern Catalonia. Many of dalio paintings were inspired by the landscapes of Catalonia. There is a strange shadow in the foreground of the painting which is a reference to Mount Pani. Dal returned to this idea with The Disintegration of the Persistence of Memory . This painting is in the Dal Museum in St. Petersburg Florida. The first Persistence of Memory is in the Museum of Modern Art in New York City. Dal also made many lithographs and sculptures of soft watches.
