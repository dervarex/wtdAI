The Pallanganmiddang also called Waveroo or Waywurru were an Indigenous Australian people from northeastern Victoria Australia. Recent studies suggest that Norman Tindale's mention of a Djilamatang tribe and language might actually refer mostly to the Pallanganmiddang or Dhudhuroa people. Many tribe and language names in this area end with parts like matong middang mirttong mathang or mittung. This ending might mean speech or tongue. For Pallanganmiddang it seems to be part of their group name. The earliest possible mention of the Pallanganmiddang might be in the s papers of George Augustus Robinson who was the Victorian Protector of Aborigines. He referred to a group called Pal ler a miiter. Pallanganmiddang is one of two languages in the Upper Murray Group the other being Dhudhuroa. It was probably spoken by people living northwest of the Dhudhuroa. The two languages share only about . of their vocabulary. A list of words called Pallanganmiddah was given by Thomas Mitchell from Tangambalanga in . A. W.Howitt said their language was very different from nearby languages and modern research agrees it was not closely related to other local languages. In Robert Smyth's map showed the Pallanganmiddang group southeast of Wodonga near Tangambalanga the northern Kiewa River Tallangatta and the Mitta Mitta River. Howitt mentioned a group called Balaung Karar southwest of Wodonga which some think refers to the same people. The Pallanganmiddang partly used the Kulin patrimoiety system which divides people into two groups eaglehawk Puntyil and crow Waang waa . This system passes group membership through the father which was common in Central Victorian Aboriginal culture but different from most of Southeast Australia. Diane Barwick found at least two clans that used Kulin names for their groups instead of Pallanganmiddang terms suggesting they married with nearby Kulin groups. Two known clans are Yowung illam balluk in the Mount Buffalo area part of the eaglehawk group Warrarak balluk near Wangaratta part of the crow group
