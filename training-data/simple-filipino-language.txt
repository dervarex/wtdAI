Filipino historically Pilipino is in practice but not according to the law a form of the Tagalog language or the Metro Manila dialect of Tagalog. It is the national language of the Philippines and one of the two official languages of the Philippines with English being the other. The Philippines is a multilingual country with living languages. These languages are spoken by different ethnic and language groups. Spanish involvement in the Philippines began in after the Spanish captured Cebu. Spain later established Manila as the capital of its colony. Manila was in a Tagalog speaking area. Before the Spanish conquest the area was ruled by the Muslim Kingdom of Luzon led by Raja Matanda and his heir Raja Sulayman and by the Hindu Buddhist Kingdom of Tondo led by Lakan Dula. After the Spanish captured Manila they made it the capital of their Asian colony. They chose Manila because of its commercial wealth influence and strategic location. Spain also wanted to protect the city from possible attacks by Portugal and the Netherlands. During the Spanish period Spanish was the official language of the government. Spanish helped unite the Philippines which contains more than islands and many different ethnic groups languages and cultures. Before Spanish rule the islands were not one united country. They were made up of independent kingdoms sultanates and tribes each with its own language and customs. During the American colonial period English became an additional official language alongside Spanish. However the number of Spanish speakers gradually decreased. The United States introduced policies that gradually removed Spanish from official use in the Philippines. Spanish was not completely banned. Instead English became the main language used in education government and law. During the American colonial period Spanish and English were official languages. However the Philippines did not have a national language at first. Article XIII Section of the Constitution which created the Commonwealth of the Philippines stated On November the first National Assembly of the Philippine Commonwealth passed Commonwealth Act No. . This law created the Institute of National Language later called the Suri n ng Wikang Pambans or SWP. The institute was ordered to study all the native languages of the Philippines and recommend one of them as the basis for a standard national language. President Manuel L. Quezon appointed representatives of the major language groups to serve on the institute. Jaime C. De Veyra became its chairman and represented the Samar Leyte Visayans. The other members were Santiago A. Fonacier who represented the Ilokano speaking regions Filemon Sotto who represented the Cebu Visayans Casimiro Perfecto who represented the Bikolanos Felix S. Sales Rodriguez who represented the Panay Visayans Hadji Butu who represented the languages of Muslim Filipinos and Cecilio Lopez who represented the Tagalogs. On November the Institute of National Language recommended Tagalog as the basis of the national language. On December President Quezon issued Executive Order No. . It approved the use of Tagalog as the basis of the national language of the Philippines. On December Quezon officially declared Tagalog to be the basis of the Wikang Pambans National Language . The decision was based on several reasons Tagalog was widely spoken and understood in many parts of the Philippines. Unlike Visayan and Bikol Tagalog was not divided into many smaller languages. Tagalog had the richest and most developed literary tradition among Philippine languages. Before more books had been written in Tagalog than in any other Philippine language. Tagalog had long been the language of Manila the political center of the Philippines and an important economic center. The Katipunan generally used Tagalog in its activities. The Philippine Revolution and the First Philippine Republic later used Spanish for official activities but many revolutionary leaders spoke Tagalog. Some non Tagalog revolutionaries and nationalists also used Tagalog in their writings especially when publishing in Manila. The Katipunan used the word Tagalog to refer to all native people of the Philippines including Cebuanos Ilocanos and Kapampangans. It also used Katagalugan to refer to the entire Philippine islands not only Tagalog speaking areas. This use supported the idea of a united opposition to Spanish rule and led to the creation of the idea of a Tagalog Republic. On June the Philippine National Assembly passed Commonwealth Act No. . It declared that the national language would become an official language on July the date when the Philippines was expected to become independent from the United States. That same year grammarian Lope K. Santos published Balar l ng Wikang Pambans . The book introduced the letter Abakada alphabet which became the standard writing system for the national language. The Institute for the Tagalog Based National Language officially adopted the alphabet. During World War II the Japanese occupiers encouraged people to use the national language instead of English in schools. As a result the Tagalog based national language was used more widely in education mass media and official communication. The census reported that people or . of the population spoke the language. This was intended to separate it from the Tagalog ethnic group. However the new name was not accepted by everyone outside the Tagalog speaking areas. This was especially true among the Cebuanos who had already opposed the decision to use Tagalog as the basis of the national language in . During the s language purists began creating new words to replace borrowed words. This movement was led by the Institute of National Language and was called purism. It was criticized by many people. Two opposing movements developed. One opposed using Tagalog as the national language while the other wanted the national language to include more words from other Philippine languages. In Negros Occidental congressman Innocencio V. Ferrer brought a case to the Supreme Court. He questioned whether choosing Tagalog as the basis of the national language was constitutional. The Supreme Court decided in favor of the national language in . Congressman Geruncio Lacuesta argued that the national language was only Tagalog and did not include enough from other Philippine languages. He later led the Modernizing the Language Approach Movement MOLAM . Lacuesta organized conferences against language purism and supported a more inclusive Manila Lingua Franca. This proposed language would accept borrowed words from both foreign and Philippine languages. Lacuesta convinced nine congressmen to propose a bill that would abolish the Institute of National Language and replace it with an Akademia ng Wikang Filipino replace the balarila with a Gramatica ng Wikang Filipino replace the letter Abakada alphabet with a letter alphabet and stop the creation of new words and the respelling of borrowed words. The movement became less active after Lacuesta died. The debate about the national language began again during the Constitutional Convention. Many delegates wanted to keep the Tagalog based national language. However most delegates who were not Tagalog favored removing the idea of a national language completely. The delegates reached a compromise. The Constitution did not remove Pilipino as the national language and it did not mention Tagalog as its basis. In both its original form and its amended form the constitution recognized English and Pilipino as official languages. It also called for the development and formal adoption of a common national language called Filipino. Filipino was intended to replace Pilipino. On August Republic Act No. created the Commission on the Filipino Language or KWF . It replaced the Institute of Philippine Languages. The KWF reports directly to the President. Its duties include studying coordinating and supporting the development use and preservation of Filipino and other Philippine languages. On May the commission issued Resolution . Filipino was given the ISO code fil. On August three regional trial courts in Malolos City Bulacan announced that they would use Filipino instead of English in court proceedings. The change was intended to promote the national language. Twelve stenographers from Branches and were selected as part of the program. They received training at the Marcelo H. del Pilar College of Law of Bulacan State University following an order from the Supreme Court. De la Rama said that Chief Justice Reynato Puno wanted to introduce the program in other areas including Laguna Cavite Quezon Aurora Nueva Ecija Batangas Rizal and Metro Manila. These areas mainly speak Tagalog as their native language. Since the Philippines has celebrated its national language for one month every August. In Filipino this celebration is called Buwan ng Wika Language Month . Before the celebration lasted one week and was called Linggo ng Wika Language Week . The celebration takes place in August because it is the birth month of President Manuel L. Quezon. He is known as the Ama ng Wikang Pambansa Father of the National Language . In Proclamation No. issued on March established a week long celebration of the national language. The celebration took place from March to April each year. April was chosen because it was the birthday of Filipino writer Francisco Baltazar the author of the Tagalog epic Florante at Laura.
