Zaza or Zazaki Zazaki zazak or Zazaki also known by other names is an Iranian language mainly spoken in eastern Turkey and is the native language of Zazas. It belongs to the northwest Iranian group of Iranic languages. The number of people who speak Zaza is not known exactly. While the language is known as Zaza or Zazaki in many Western sources the language or dialects has been known by a variety of other names by the people speaking it. One popular name is Dimili or Dimli Zazaki dimil D m l also known by some speakers as dimbil . The term is believed to either come from a region in Northern Iran known as Daylam or the Kurdish Donboli tribe also known as D mbeli which has existed in regions where Zaza is spoken. Some scholars have researched that the word Dimili might from a region in Northern Iran known as Daylam due to the similarity to languages in Daylam like Talysh or Semnani. Other scholars disagree with this and say that evidence is weak when you look at other things than language. Zazas were also known as Tmbli in some Armenian sources and the sound changes that could turn a word like Dunbuli into Dimili is also documented. Another popular name is Kirmanjki Zazaki kirmanck or K rmancki which should not be confused with Kurmanji. They do have the same etymology. The word kirmanck K rmancki comes from kirmanc K rmanc which generally means a Kurd or an Alevi Kurd. Some researchers believe it only has the meaning of an Alevi but other researchers believe that the term can have multiple meanings at the same time. Another name for the language is Kirdki Zazaki kirdk or K rdki which means Kurdish. Zaza is classified as a Northwestern Iranian language and is according to Glottolog a part of the Adharic languages and is closely related to the languages here. It has traditionally been considered a Kurdish dialect but especially due to early phonological sound related differences a better description would be a language rather than a dialect. However Zaza is phonologically very similar to Kurmanji and has been highly influenced by it through history. It has sometimes been considered to be a part of the Zaza Gorani language group. A researcher Shuan Karim also reconstructed Proto Zaza Gorani grammar in a peer reviewed presentation at ICIL . According to Jost Gippert it might be especially close with Gorani and Semnani because of similar grammar.
