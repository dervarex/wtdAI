The Polynesian languages are a group of languages spoken in Oceania. They all belong in the Malayo Polynesian branch of the Austronesian languages. They are mostly spoken in Polynesia but some are spoken in nearby Melanesia and Micronesia. There are around Polynesian languages with Samoan having the most speakers. Other well known Polynesian languages include M ori Tongan Hawaiian and Tahitian. The Polynesian languages formed when Austronesians in New Caledonia the Lapita culture started moving to other parts of Oceania. Navigation of Oceania continued until AD with the discovery of New Zealand Aotearoa by the M ori people. Today there are over million Polynesians although speakers of Polynesian languages number far less due to historical reasons such as disease and colonialism. Throughout the Polynesian languages many sound changes occur. They mainly exist in consonants. For example Samoan f corresponds to Hawaiian h .
