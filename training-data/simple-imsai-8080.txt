The IMSAI was an early microcomputer begun in late based on the Intel and later and the S bus. It was compatible with its main competitor the earlier MITS Altair by which it was inspired. The IMSAI is largely thought of as the first clone computer. The IMSAI machine used a highly modified version of the CP M operating system called IMDOS. It was designed made and sold by IMS Associates Inc. later renamed IMSAI Manufacturing Corp . In total between and units were produced from until . In May William Millard began business as IMS Associates IMS in the area of computer consultancy and engineering using his home as an office. By Millard began IMS Associates Inc. Millard soon found capital for his business and received several contracts all for software. In IMS was contacted by a client which wanted a workstation system that could do jobs for any General Motors new car dealership. IMS planned a system including a terminal small computer printer and special software. Five of these work stations were to have common access to a hard disk which would be controlled by a small computer. Eventually product development was stopped. Millard and his chief engineer Joe Killian turned to the microprocessor. Intel had announced the chip and compared to the to which IMS Associates had first used the looked like a better idea. Full scale development of the IMSAI was begun and by October an advertisement was placed in the magazine Popular Electronics receiving positive reactions. IMS shipped the first IMSAI kits on December . In IMS was renamed to IMSAI Manufacturing Corporation because by then they were a manufacturing company not a consultancy one. By October the IMSAI corporation had gone bankrupt and the 'IMSAI' trademark was acquired by Thomas Todd Fischer and Nancy Freitas former early employees of IMS Associates who continued manufacturing the computers under the IMSAI name as a division of Fischer Freitas Co. Support for early IMSAI systems continues to this day. The IMSAI was used for Small business data processing applications Data communications and data entry systems Scientific applications Computer sciences education and development Banking and insurance applications Military and general government applications Personal computer systems An IMSAI and an acoustic coupler type modem were among the hacking tools used by the main character in the movie WarGames. However even by the acoustic coupler was out of date. It was selected over a more modern model so that the audience would immediately recognize it as the device that connected to the telephone.
