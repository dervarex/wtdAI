Ottawa or Odawa is a dialect of the Ojibwe language spoken by the Odawa people in southern Ontario in Canada and northern Michigan in the United States. The first recorded meeting of Ottawa speakers and Europeans occurred in when a party of Ottawas met explorer Samuel de Champlain on the north shore of Georgian Bay. Ottawa is written in Latin letters. The dialect is known to its speakers as speaking the native language or speaking Ottawa . People who speak Ottawa are concerned that their language is endangered because the use of English increases and the number of speakers declines. Most Ottawa speaking groups are in Ontario. Places in Ontario where Ottawa is spoken include Walpole Island near Detroit and Wikwemikong on Manitoulin Island in Lake Huron. Places in Michigan where Ottawa is spoken are Peshawbestown Harbor Springs Grand Rapids Mount Pleasant Bay City and Cross Village. Some children of Ottawas who moved to Kansas and Oklahoma speak Ottawa. Only three older people spoke Ottawa in Oklahoma in .
