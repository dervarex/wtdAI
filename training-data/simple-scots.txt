Scots is a West Germanic language. It is sometimes called Lowland Scots or Lallans. It is not a subdialect of Scottish English but the two are similar. Scottish English is a dialect of English and Scots is a separate language. Ulster Scots is a dialect of Scots found in the north of Ireland. Scots is very different from the Scottish Gaelic language which is a Celtic language. There have been disagreements about the linguistic historical and social status of Scots. Scots is at one end of a scale with Scottish English at the other. Scots is generally regarded as one of the ancient varieties of Scottish English and has its own distinct variants such as Doric. Northumbrian Old English was established in what is now southeastern Scotland as far as the River Forth by the seventh century. The region was part of the Anglo Saxon kingdom of Northumbria. Early Scots began to diverge from Northumbrian English in the twelfth and thirteenth century. There was immigration of Scandinavian influenced Middle English speakers from the North and Midlands of England. Later influences on the development of Scots were from Romance languages via ecclesiastical and legal Latin Norman and later Parisian French from the Auld Alliance and Dutch or Middle Low German influences by trade and immigration from the Low Countries. Scots also includes loanwords from contact with Gaelic. From the th century Early Scots spread further into Scotland through the burghs established by King David I. The growth in prestige of Early Scots in the th century and the decline of French in Scotland made Scots the prestige dialect in most of eastern Scotland. From to the s during the Plantation of Ulster many Scots speaking Lowlanders about settled there. In the core areas of Scots settlement there were five or six times as many Scots as English settlers. Southern Modern English was adopted as the literary language after and Modern Scots is sometimes used to describe the spoken language after .
