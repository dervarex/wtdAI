The kernel of a group homomorphism from G to H is the subset of the elements from G that arrive to the Identity element of H. Mathematically ker f g in G f g e H . Since a group homomorphism preserves identity elements the identity element of G must belong to the kernel subset. The homomorphism is injective if and only if its kernel is only the identity elements of G. Proof If f were not injective then the non injective elements can form a distinct element of its kernel there would exist a b in G such that a neq b and f a f b . Thus f a f b e H. f is a group homomorphism so inverses and group operations are preserved giving f left ab right e H in other words ab in ker f and ker f would not be the singleton. Conversely distinct elements of the kernel violate injectivity directly if there would exist an element g neq e G in ker f then f g f e G e H thus f would not be injective.
