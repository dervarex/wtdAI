The Sino Tibetan or Trans Himalayan languages are a language family spoken in Asia. The family includes Chinese the Tibeto Burman languages and about other languages of East Asia. Kra Dai languages and Hmong Mien languages are also sometimes included. The largest language group in this family is the Chinese languages with more than . billion native speakers. It also has the oldest known writing in the family using hanzi going back to the Jiahu symbols in BC. All of these languages descend from a single proto language. Researchers are still working to understand what this language may have sounded like. Some researchers think the Sino Tibetan languages most likely came from the Huanghe region of north central China Zhongyuan . Others think they came from farther west in southwest China or even Northeast India. Proto Chinese and Proto Tibeto Burman had many different prefixes and suffixes. Proto Chinese changed into Old Chinese around the time of the Shang Dynasty. This can be seen in the Book of Songs. Nouns verbs and modifiers depended on affixes at the beginnings or ends of words such as s p and k. After the Warring States period in China Old Chinese started using tones. The suffix s was also used. The usual word order in Sino Tibetan languages is subject object verb. Modern Chinese Bai Karenic and Mruic are exceptions. Subject object verb order was likely the original word order. Over time Chinese changed to subject verb object order. However Chinese differs from almost all other verb object languages because it places relative clauses before the nouns they describe. Sino Tibetan may be related to the Altaic languages.
