Meitei script or Meetei script is an abugida used for Meitei language officially known as Manipuri language the sole official language the lingua franca and the predominantly spoken tongue of the Indian state of Manipur. The official Meitei writing system consists of the following numerals known as Cheising Eeyek. primary characters that is constituted by initial letters known as Eeyek Eepee and additional letters known as Lom Eeyek. derived characters from Eeyek Eepee known as Lonsum Eeyek. associating symbols known as Cheitap Eeyek. punctuation marks called Khudam Mayek. Early form of Meitei script also called Old Manipuri script was evolved from the Gupta Brahmi script also known as Gupta script or Late Brahmi script . The original Brahmi script underwent modifications to accommodate the phonological distinctions of the Meitei language. The Gupta Brahmi descended scripts were later categorized into four groups in which Meitei script is put under the Tibetan group along with the eponymous Tibetan script and other sister scripts. The ancestral Gupta script of th century even passed through the lands of Assam and Manipur and reached further eastwards into Myanmar thereby developing into other writing systems in the th century. Old Meitei script has certain visual similarities or resemblance with the Old Burmese script also called Old Myanmarese script and the Thai script which are also derived from the Brahmi script. The use of writing systems in Tibeto Burman languages appears to be very restricted. Meitei or Manipuri is one of four Tibeto Burman languages with an old written tradition the other three being Tibetan Burmese and Newar . The Meitei script was evident in the Yumbanlol copper plate inscriptions dated to the period between CE which was one of the oldest surviving Meitei literary works. It was originally composed during the old historical period of ancient Kangleipak early Manipur when the Meitei script and ancient Meitei language were already long established in official and cultural heritage.
