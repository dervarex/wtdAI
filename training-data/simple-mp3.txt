MPEG Audio Layer III or MPEG Audio Layer III MP is a lossy data compression format for audio. Using it makes smaller computer files containing digitized music and other sounds. Many people copy music into MP files so they can listen to it on their computer or a small portable MP player. In the s Dieter Seitzer started the development of a code for audio format of high quality. Together with his team at the German Fraunhofer Institut in Erlangen the professor had the idea to create code with a low bit rate. The German institute received the patent for MP in . MP was then passed to the International Organisation for Standardisation. In the same year the MP was included into MPEG specification. MPEG means Moving Picture Experts Group . The first player for the MP format was designed by the same Fraunhofer Institute in 's. A Croatian student named Tomislav Uzelac developed AMP MP Playback Engine in . After the player appeared on the Internet two students named Justin Frankel and Dmitry Boldyrev transformed it into Winamp. They took the engine of the original player and added the interface of the Windows. When Winamp was launched into the net for free use it became successful at once. This way began the era of free music download. Soon various programmers started creating additional features for the MP player. Very often they created new encoders rippers software for transforming audio tracks into MP files and players. Because there is no need to pay licensing fees in order to build the player today many software MP players are free. Some companies claim to have patents covering the MP format but the validity of these patents is questionable because the format specification was published more than years ago meaning that any relevant patents would have expired. The most famous MP players are Winamp Sonique iTunes and MusicMatch. The main feature of an MP player is to turn the MP files into standard audio form and then send them to the soundcard of the computer. The soundcard then outputs the files into speaker so the user can hear them. Every MP player interprets music differently though each one of them uses the same code for playing the MP files. There are also stand alone without a computer MP players. These may be portable or they may be built into a stereo system or car stereo. According to the MPEG standards MP players are supposed to also be able to play MP and MP files although many don't. The MP format is a lossy compression format. This means that each time something is compressed with it some information is lost. This information can not be recovered. When encoding to MP converting some audio data to it the encoder deletes some unimportant parts. The human ear cannot hear certain sounds if they are masked by other sounds. This means that when encoding certain sound samples some sounds can be left out as they will not be heard. This makes it possible to compress audio by a factor of about without noticeable change to the sound of the music. With slight audible changes to the sound compression factors of about are possible. When compressing for each frame a small section of audio the encoder first splits the audio into different parts using a filter bank similar to MP . The audio is then split further using the modified discrete cosine transform into different parts or different parts if there is a sudden signal change such as a loud drum beat. The MP encoder then removes parts that it thinks the human ear cannot hear. The remaining parts are then compressed using Huffman coding. Decompression does these same steps except removal of parts of the audio in reverse. When an MP file or stream is encoded the user selects a particular bitrate. This can be either a constant bitrate the bitrate is the same throughout the file or a variable bitrate the bitrate is higher in more complex parts of the song and lower in simple parts. MP can have a bitrate up to kbit s. Higher bitrates such as to kbit s produce larger files but the sound quality is better. Lower bitrates such as kbit s are useful for streaming over the internet but the quality is not as good. The MPEG standard allows bitrates up to kbps but using kbps at the standard CD sample rate of . KHz creates frames that are larger than the standard allows. Most players will still play these files. The highest bitrate at . KHz that is strictly within the standard is kbps. This is not an issue at KHz. Some older players will not play variable bitrate VBR files or files that are more than kbps. Some players will play VBR files but will show incorrect track length or not show the track length at all. Other formats for audio compression are Ogg Vorbis AAC and FLAC. Ogg Vorbis and AAC are very similar to MP although they have many improvements for example they encode noisy signals such as tape hiss more efficiently and they don't use the MP style filter bank.
