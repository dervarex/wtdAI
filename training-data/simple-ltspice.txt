LTspice is a free computer program used to test electronic circuits. An engineer draws a circuit on the screen and the program works out how it will behave before it is built. This kind of program is called a circuit simulator. It is based on a program called SPICE and is one of the most widely used circuit simulators. LTspice was first made by Linear Technology and is now made by Analog Devices. The program is free and unlike some free programs it does not limit how large or complex a circuit can be. It comes with a library of ready made parts such as resistors diodes and transistors that people can drop into their circuits. LTspice does not design the layout of a printed circuit board which is the physical board that holds the parts. It is used to test how a circuit works not to lay one out. The first public version came out in . At that time it was called SwitcherCAD III. A new version LTspice IV came out in . A version for Apple macOS computers followed in . LTspice XVII came out in . In Linear Technology became part of Analog Devices and parts made by Analog Devices were added to the program.
