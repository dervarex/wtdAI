Christopher Poole born known online as moot is an American Internet entrepreneur. Best known for creating the English language imageboard chan in October . He served as the site's head administrator for more than years before stepping down in January . In he began working for Google. As of he works in the company's Google Maps division based in Tokyo. In he left Google. In April Poole was voted the world's most influential person of by an open Internet poll conducted by Time. On September Poole gave a talk on why chan has a reputation as a Meme Factory at the Paraflows Symposium in Vienna Austria which was part of the Paraflows festival themed Urban Hacking. In this talk Poole mainly attributed this to the anonymous system and to the lack of data retention on the site The site has no memory . His talk was published in the academic reader Mind and Matter Comparative Approaches towards Complexity edited by G nther Friesinger Johannes Grenzfurthner Thomas Ballhausen . On February Poole spoke at the TED conference in Long Beach California. He spoke about the increasing prevalence of persistent user identities and the sharing of personal information on sites such as Facebook and Twitter and he also spoke about the value of anonymous posting on sites such as chan. Fred Leal of the Brazilian newspaper O Estado de S. Paulo said his inclusion in the conference indicates that something extraordinary is happening challenges every Internet convention it is alone the antithesis of Google social networking sites and blogs. In Poole was reported to have raised to create a new online enterprise Canvas. The website opened on January and featured digitally modified images uploaded by users who are required to self identify using Facebook Connect. The enterprise ran until January when Poole announced that Canvas and its DrawQuest feature would be going out of business. In January Poole announced that he would be stepping down as the chan administrator. On January he hosted a final Q A with site users using the qa board and YouTube to livestream. This marked the beginning of his retirement from being an administrator and owner of the website after eleven and a half years. He began a process of turning control of the site over to three anonymous chan moderators while searching for a buyer for the website. On September it was announced that Hiroyuki Nishimura founder of the Japanese BBS channel would take over as the site's owner.
