The Macintosh Plus is a personal computer that was designed manufactured and sold by Apple Computer from January to October . Introduced two years after the original Macintosh it was the third model in the Macintosh line and featured several hardware improvements over its predecessor the Macintosh K. The Macintosh Plus shipped with MB of RAM expandable to MB via four pin SIMM slots a new KB floppy drive with two sides and a SCSI port. The SCSI interface allowed the system to connect to external hard drives printers scanners and other peripherals the first time Apple had done this. It was also the first Macintosh to support the HFS for more complex file management. The Macintosh Plus introduced some improvements including a SCSI port for external devices MB of RAM and an KB double sided floppy disk drive. Its improved RAM capacity allowed it to run more demanding software and the SCSI port made it popular in desktop publishing and business applications. The Plus maintained the all in one form factor like the computers before it with a inch monochrome display and small size. It came bundled with a keyboard featuring arrow keys and a numeric keypad. Like earlier models the Macintosh Plus didn't have an internal fan which made it quieter than competing PCs but also prone to overheating. On the inside of the case the names of the original Macintosh development team were molded into the plastic including Steve Jobs Andy Hertzfeld and Susan Kare. The Macintosh Plus originally shipped with System Software . and Finder . and it remained compatible up to System . . . It included software bundles such as MacPaint and MacWrite HyperCard Microsoft Excel first released on Macintosh and Aldus PageMaker. Apple offered a factory upgrade kit for owners of earlier Macs K or K allowing them to convert to a Plus by replacing the motherboard floppy drive and back case. Some third part developers released accelerators and memory expansions that pushed RAM up to MB far above Apple's official MB maximum. Early Macintosh Plus units had a SCSI bug that would prevent booting when external devices were not powered correctly. The lack of a cooling fan led to overheating in some units with long term strain on analog board solder joints and power supply failures being common. The Macintosh Plus was in production for nearly five years longer than any other Macintosh until the second generation Mac Pro . It was a common to see one at schools and universities throughout the late s. Some educational units were sold under the name Macintosh Plus ED for institutional use.
