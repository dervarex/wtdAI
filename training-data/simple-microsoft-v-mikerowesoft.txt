Microsoft v. MikeRoweSoft was a legal case in . It was between the company Microsoft and a year old student from Canada named Mike Rowe. Rowe made a website called MikeRoweSoft.com. The name sounded like Microsoft . Microsoft said this was too close to their company name and asked him to give it up. Mike Rowe made the website in for his small web design business. He chose the name as a joke using his real name and adding soft . Microsoft said this broke their trademark and first offered him to give up the name. Rowe asked for instead. Microsoft then sent him a long legal letter. They said he was cybersquatting which means using a name to get money from a big company. The story became popular in the news. Many people supported Rowe. His website got so many visitors that it went offline. Microsoft got bad publicity and later agreed to a deal. Rowe gave Microsoft the website name. In return he got an Xbox with games a trip to Microsoft in Redmond Washington help with Microsoft training money to pay his costs a subscription to a Microsoft developer program He made a new website called MikeRoweForums.com. Rowe also gave most of the money people sent him to a children's charity called Canuck Place. After the case Rowe sold the legal papers on eBay. Some experts said Microsoft had to protect their trademark. Others said Rowe could have won because he was using his real name.
