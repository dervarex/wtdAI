A video codec is a piece of software or hardware that makes video or audio smaller. The word 'codec' is a blend between 'encoder' thing that makes the video smaller and 'decoder' thing that puts video to the screen . Compressed smaller data is usually put to a certain format. The data is normally lossy the data lacks some details which means that a video that is put through a decoder is usually blurry or low quality because there is not enough data to rebuild the video properly. There are relationships between the quality of the video the bitrate amount of data used for video and how complex the methods the codec uses. There is also a thought for latency delay between both ends. That is usually a problem only when the video is played over the Internet. Previously video was stored on magnetic tape which has an analog signal until the introduction of the CD. The CD was the first time it made sense to store and play video in digital form. Because of the large size to store raw video a method was needed to reduce the amount of data used. In a compression method called DCT short for discrete cosine transform was made by Nasir Ahmed T. Natarajan and K. R. Rao. During the s some companies began to test DCT to store video which led to the creation of the H. standard. The H. standard was the first time that a video codec was practical. It was developed by multiple companies such as Hitachi and Toshiba. DCT has been used by all of the big video coding standards since then. Some of the most popular standards for codecs have been the MPEG standards. MPEG was made in and was made to compress VHS quality video. It was improved in when MPEG sometimes called H. was made. It was made by lots of companies but the ones that helped the most were Sony Thomson and Mitsubishi. MPEG became the normal format for DVD and low quality digital TV. In MPEG was released which was much better than previous video compression technology. The most used codec as of is H. . It was made in by many companies such as Panasonic and LG.
