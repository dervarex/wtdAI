Maltese is the language of Malta and a language of the European Union. Maltese sounds similar to the Phoenician language that was spoken in areas around the ancient Mediterranean. It is written in the Latin alphabet like English. Maltese descends from a dialect of Arabic called Siculo Arabic. The language has borrowed many words from Sicilian Italian and English. Around people speak Maltese. Most of them live in Malta. Many people from Sicily settled in Malta after the Muslims conquered it in AD. They spoke a Sicilian Arabic language from the Maghreb. In later centuries their language changed into Maltese. The oldest known document in Maltese is Il Cantilena a poem from the th century written by Pietro Caxaro. For centuries Maltese was mainly a spoken language. Maltese became an official language of Malta in alongside English. Before that year the only official language of Malta was Italian. Italian however is still used a lot in the media. Italian and English words are being taken in by the language more and more. This sometimes causes words to form that are not found in Maltese Italian or English but instead are a mix of the three. At schools English is taught as a second language. Maltese is usually used in the standardized jobs and when people talk to each other. Most of the television radio and literature in Malta is also in Maltese.
