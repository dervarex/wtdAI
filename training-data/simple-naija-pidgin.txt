Naija Pidgin or Nigerian Pidgin is an English based creole language spoken as a lingua franca language across Nigeria. The language is sometimes referred to as Pijin or Vernacular and it has over time become the basic language in Nigeria. It came into existence during the th and th centuries as a result of contact between Britons and Africans involved in the Atlantic slave trade in the s a common orthography was developed for Pidgin which has been gaining significant popularity in giving the language a harmonized writing system. It can be spoken as a creole or a dialect by different speakers who may switch between these forms depending on their social setting. Variations of what this article refers to as Nigerian Pidgin are also spoken across West and Central Africa in countries such as Benin Ghana and Cameroon. Nigerian Pidgin is commonly used throughout the country and across all ethnic groups but it does not have official status. In the BBC started BBC News Pidgin to provide services in Pidgin. Naija Pidgin varies by location which also determines the influencing substrate language affecting the superstrate English structure resulting in varied flavours. Dialects of Nigerian Pidgin include The Sapele Warri Ughelli dialect spoken in the western Niger Delta area which is also sometimes grouped together with the Benin City dialect that has influences from the Bini language due to their geographical closeness and common features The Port Harcourt dialect of Rivers State and the eastern Niger Delta The Lagos or Western dialect which is highly influenced by Yoruba language and the Onitsha or Eastern variety that draws influences from Igbo language. According to Obiechina Nigerian Pidgin English can be subdivided into five dialectal areas or variants Eastern Pidgin Port Harcourt Kalabari Onitsha Owerri Aba Etc. South West Pidgin Lagos Ibadan Etc. Northern Pidgin Kaduna Kano Maiduguri Jos Etc. South Central Pidgin Abraka Warri Sapele Agbor Etc. Cross River Pidgin Calabar Uyo Ikot Ekpene Obolo Etc.
