Older fourth generation fighters were mostly built for high maneuverability and close range dogfighting . Fifth generation fighters have different features. These features usually include stealth which means weapons munitions are stored inside the plane to make it harder to detect. They also have high maneuverability and are often able to take off and land on short runways. Another feature is supercruise which is the ability to fly faster than sound for a long time without using reheat. They also have advanced electronics avionics like low probability of intercept radar LPIR . They use networked data fusion which combines information from many sources to give the pilot better situation awareness of the battlefield. Finally they are multirole meaning they can do many jobs at once such as helping with command control and communications C . To make them harder to see on radar a small radar cross section or RCS most fifth generation fighters use chines instead of standard leading edge extensions. They also usually do not have canards small wings near the nose . However the Sukhoi T has parts near its engine intakes that act somewhat like canards. The designers of the Chengdu J decided to use canards to make the plane more agile even though this can make it less stealthy. These fighters also have two vertical tails that are tilted like a V tail to help reduce radar detection from the side. The fighters that have supermaneuverability usually get this ability by using thrust vectoring. All of these fighters have internal weapon bays spaces inside the plane to carry weapons. This helps them avoid using weapon racks pylons on the outside which make the plane easier to see on radar. However they still have external hardpoints on their wings. These can be used for missions where stealth is not needed or for carrying extra fuel tanks like the F does when flying to a new base. All fifth generation fighters are also made of a lot of composite materials. This helps reduce their weight and their radar signature RCS . All fifth generation fighters that we know about use regular commercial off the shelf COTS main computer processors. These processors control all the plane's sensors. The sensors' information is combined sensor fusion to create one clear picture of the battlefield for the pilot using both the plane's own sensors and information from other friendly forces. In older jet fighters each sensor or piece of equipment would give its own separate information and the pilot had to mentally combine all that information to understand the battlefield. For example when the F A was first delivered it did not have synthetic aperture radar SAR or an infra red search and track system for situational awareness. It is getting the SAR ability later through software updates. However a problem in these very complex software systems can shut down other parts of the plane that seem unrelated. The complexity of an aircraft that depends on software can lead to big software problems software crisis causing extra costs and delays.
